6. Objections, with the honest answer
They say
You say
"Is it a real person?"
No, and it says so on every call. Callers get a clear message taken and confirmed instead of voicemail. Watch the demo and judge it yourself.
"Can it book appointments?"
Yes, with the Calendar Integration add-on, $129 one-time: the caller picks an open slot on your calendar during the call and it is confirmed before they hang up. Without the add-on it takes the message with the caller's preferred way to be reached, and you book.
"What does it cost me monthly, really?"
$359 to us, which includes 300 minutes of AI answering. Your phone number and its call minutes are billed by Twilio to your own account, usually a few dollars a month. Your domain stays in your name too.
"Can I keep my number?"
Yes. Keep it and forward it, or port it. Porting takes days and has paperwork; forwarding is same-day. The implementer walks you through either.
"Who owns my accounts?"
You own your domain, your phone number, your email sending domain, and your payment processor. We run the hosting, database, and AI. We never hold your passwords; you issue us a key you can revoke.
"What happens if you disappear?"
Two things are built in. Your phone line has a fallback that rings your own number if our service is ever inactive, set up in your own Twilio account. And the terms give you written notice, a 30-day window, and a one-time export of your site, leads, and messages before anything is shut down.
"Is my callers' information safe?"
No call audio is stored anywhere. The transcript is held at the voice provider only while you are a client. The database keeps only what a caller deliberately leaves: name, number, message. The privacy page on our site says exactly this.
"Can it text my customers back?"
No. It texts you: every new message is sent to your on-call phone as well as your dashboard. It does not text your customers; you reply.
"Can I cancel?"
Month to month after the build. No refund for a change of mind on delivered work; if we fail to deliver, you get back what you paid for work not done. The terms page says this in plain English.
"Do you provide leads or guarantee results?"
No, to both. We answer the phone and capture what comes in.
"Why not just use [a cheaper AI answering app]?"
Compare on the demo recording, then on what happens when it goes wrong: the failover to your own number, no stored audio, a person who installed it and supports it, and a dashboard your messages land in.

9. Close (Stage 3), and what happens after
Three exit criteria, no exceptions: contract signed (Bridge Builders signs it, not you), payment taken by Bridge Builders, and the packet complete. Then the implementer takes it. You are reintroduced at go-live and own the relationship from there; the implementer owns the system. The client should know which of you is which before they need either.
You are paid on collection, not on signature: 25% of the implementation fee at the base tier (30% at 11 deals in a month, 35% at 21, retroactive within the month), and 16.4% of the monthly fee for as long as the client stays, while you are a partner. On a Receptionist deal that is $325 once and $59 a month. Paid by the 10th of the following month with a statement of every deal and residual. Attribution is recorded once, when the onboarding record is created; two partners, one prospect, the earlier record wins. The only clawback is a refunded implementation fee, which is rare by design.
10. Staying lawful
You are responsible for how you source leads. The short version:
Cold calls: no autodialers, no recorded messages, honor the national Do Not Call registry for consumer lines, business-to-business calling during business hours is the norm.
Email: every message carries your real physical address and a working opt-out; opt-outs are honored the same day; no purchased or scraped lists whose terms forbid it.
Texting: only where the recipient has given consent or has an existing business relationship with you. Cold texting to a mobile number is the fastest way to a complaint.
Do not use Bridge Builders' name in any campaign the owner has not approved in writing.
Keep records of consent where the law requires them.
