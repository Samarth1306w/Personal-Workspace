Not now. Health care and medical practices, legal advice, financial advice, emergency services, services aimed at children under 13 (below), and anything regulated. Those need a separate review before Bridge Builders sells into them. If a prospect is one of these, say so plainly and stop.

Children's and youth businesses. The test is who calls, chats or uses the website, not what the business does. A service can be for children without being aimed at them. Under the federal children's privacy law (COPPA), a child's voice or message is personal information, and collecting it from a child under 13 generally needs a parent's consent first. The Receptionist and the Assistant keep what people say to them, so this matters.

Not aimed at children, so you can sell it. Parents or adults do the buying, calling and chatting, even though the kids get the service:

  • A batting-cage and training facility like D-BAT: cage rentals, lessons and memberships bought by adults, and a website written for parents and adult players.
  • A travel ball or club organization: tryouts, fees and registration handled by parents.
  • A lessons or training business where parents call to book sessions for their kids.

For these, answer Yes to the regulated-callers question (it lists minors) when you fill the handoff packet. A child may still call now and then, and the install sets what the Receptionist does when one does, such as asking for a parent to call back.

Aimed at children, so not now:

  • A kids' camp or clinic website written to the kids ("Hey sluggers, sign up for summer camp!"), with cartoon mascots or games, where the kids fill in forms or use the chat themselves.
  • A youth league where players of about 8 to 12 log in, check schedules or send messages on their own.
  • A phone line kids are told to call themselves ("Players, call to find out if practice is on").

The quick test: who will call or chat? Parents or adults, sell it and flag it in the packet. Children under 13 using it themselves, not now.

The after-hours test. Call a prospect's line after 6pm. If a person answers, move on; they have it covered. If it goes to voicemail, you have the opening line for tomorrow morning, and it is true.

5. What you may not say

  • That booking is included. It is the $129 Calendar Integration add-on, and only that. Never say it routes calls, transfers to a person, speaks other languages, or texts customers.
  • That it sounds human, speaks in the owner's voice, or "callers won't know."
  • Any dollar figure of savings or revenue, any guaranteed callback time, any guarantee at all.
  • That Bridge Builders' products, contracts, or processes are attorney reviewed, compliant, certified, or risk free.
  • That you are an employee, officer, or owner of Bridge Builders. You are an "independent sales partner for Bridge Builders."
  • Any price other than the public one, any discount, any custom scope, any timeline you did not get from the implementer. You do not sign anything and you do not take payment.

8. Discovery (Stage 2): the handoff packet

The implementer builds from what you gather. Every item exists because an install stalls without it, and the implementer has the right to return an incomplete packet with the gaps named. The packet lives in Bridge Builders' onboarding record, which enforces the required fields per product and shows why each one matters. Until you have a login to it, send the answers by email to the owner, who enters them; the record is still the system of record.

Every product: legal name and DBA; physical address and whether it may be shown; service area; whether anything about the business or its callers is regulated; business hours, time zone, holidays; the named technical contact who will create accounts and click buttons; who approves go-live; who signs off content; the BridgeView owner email; which products at which prices; and the client's own definition of "done."

Website: domain owned or not, registrar, who controls DNS today (or five preferred names); new site or replacing one; pages, homepage summary, call to action, About and Services direction; logo, brand color, tone, hero image, asset ownership; public email and phone.

Assistant: the approved knowledge (services, policies, FAQs); tone; lead-alert email; handoff contact and the response time to promise; if standalone, the existing site and who controls it.

Receptionist: current number and whether to forward, port, or take a new one; the fallback number, a number of theirs, never ours; who answers today and after hours; approved knowledge (what it may and may not say); who receives messages and how; greeting direction; who at the client will hold the Twilio account.

9. Close (Stage 3), and what happens after

Three exit criteria, no exceptions: contract signed (Bridge Builders signs it, not you), payment taken by Bridge Builders, and the packet complete. Then the implementer takes it. You are reintroduced at go-live and own the relationship from there; the implementer owns the system. The client should know which of you is which before they need either.

You are paid on collection, not on signature: 25% of the implementation fee at the base tier (30% at 11 deals in a month, 35% at 21, retroactive within the month), and 16.4% of the monthly fee for as long as the client stays, while you are a partner. On a Receptionist deal that is $325 once and $59 a month. Paid by the 10th of the following month with a statement of every deal and residual. Attribution is recorded once, when the onboarding record is created; two partners, one prospect, the earlier record wins. The only clawback is a refunded implementation fee, which is rare by design.

10. Staying lawful

You are responsible for how you source leads. The short version:

  • Cold calls: no autodialers, no recorded messages, honor the national Do Not Call registry for consumer lines, business-to-business calling during business hours is the norm.
  • Email: every message carries your real physical address and a working opt-out; opt-outs are honored the same day; no purchased or scraped lists whose terms forbid it.
  • Texting: only where the recipient has given consent or has an existing business relationship with you. Cold texting to a mobile number is the fastest way to a complaint.
  • Do not use Bridge Builders' name in any campaign the owner has not approved in writing.
  • Keep records of consent where the law requires them.
